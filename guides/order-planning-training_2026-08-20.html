Order Planning Training — Review

Draft Review Updated 2026-09-03

Read the summary

Objective

Introduce order planning — MRP — to the trainees, using the bamboo test-item family (BAM-CHOP-PAIR, BAM-ROD, BAM-SLEEVE) already built in the 2026-08-18 walkthrough. The session answers a question asked directly on the call: "have you already used planning?" — and works forward from what an item needs before it can be planned at all, through where demand comes from, to how the order horizon decides which demand planning is willing to see.

This is the first recording in the library to cover Enterprise Planning. Every previous session stopped at execution level — sales orders, production orders, warehouse receipts — where supply was created by hand.

Key topics discussed

3.1 An item cannot be planned until it has planning data

The session opened by searching Item Order Plan cprrp0520m000 for BAM-CHOP-PAIR and finding nothing: it's not set up for planning yet03:45.

The reasoning given is exact and worth keeping: This is one of the main planning screens, so it uses the items planning data. But if it's not here then you can't plan it04:39. The link between an item and its planning data is the cluster — "under clusters in the item master, that's where items planning is linked to the overall item."

The item's group, BAM-FG "Bamboo Finished Goods", had been created new for the bamboo family, so the Item Defaults template carried no cluster and the planning record was not generated automatically. It was created manually instead, after which the item appeared in the Item Order Plan (05:41).

The environment's planning cluster is `NA1` ("Cluster NA 1") and the site is `CSADNA01` ("PCG CSAD NA Site 1 Manufacturi Houston,TX") — both read from the selection block of Planned Orders cprrp1100m000.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file, the source on file

The vendor states the concept behind this: a planning cluster "represents one or more business locations for which planning processes are performed", and "if the multisite concepts are activated, planning clusters and sites are mandatory" — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Planning Clusters — "Planning clusters in Enterprise Planning" (p. 39). That a cluster was genuinely required here, rather than defaulting blank, is evidence that multisite is active in this environment — see section 6.

3.2 Demand has to exist, and the item has to be sellable

If there's no demand there won't be anything to plan. Or if there's not demand and sufficient supply, there's nothing to plan06:25. A sales order was duplicated to create demand.

Two execution-level obstacles surfaced before the demand could be booked, both worth recording as procedure:

  1. An item cannot go on a sales order without a sales-office record for the order's sales office. The error message was read out as "item sales by sales office not present". The fix was to add the row on the Sales Offices subtab of Item tcibd0501m100, taking the sales office from the order header.
  2. After a master-data change, the session must be exited and re-entered before it will pick the change up — with these things, when you do like a master data change like I just did, you got to exit that session, come back in so that it'll do a refresh07:06.

The order was booked for 5 ea against 1 ea on hand — that means that there's four short07:44.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file, which also shows the item's Order System as Planned and the full subentity set (Sales, Production, Service, Ordering, Warehousing selected; Project, Purchase, Quality, Tool not).

3.3 The order horizon decides which demand planning sees

This was the core teaching point, and it was demonstrated as a controlled experiment rather than asserted.

With the sales order's delivery date set to today, the demand appeared in the Item Order Plan. Moved out to a week from now and refreshed, it disappeared: it's gone. Why is it gone? This means planning does not recognize that demand08:25. The trainees were asked to explain it and could not — so that is your horizons09:05.

The screen at that moment shows why. The Horizons block of cprrp0520m000 reads Order Horizon `8/20/2026` — the day of the recording — with Planning Horizon and CTP Horizon on the same date, and an empty grid.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file

The order horizon was then extended in Items - Planning cprpd1100m000 and the plan refreshed. The same session now shows Order Horizon `8/28/2026` and two rows dated 8/27/2026: the Sales Order 100000667 with Demand 5.0000, and a Planned Production Order 000000061 with Supply 4.0000 and Planned Available 5.0000 — MRP's answer to five short of one on hand.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file

The definition given was the horizon is how far out you want planning to consider demand for an item10:05, and the business reason was given plainly:

If you had an order horizon of infinity, and you had a demand that's a year from now or two years from now, then planning is going to tell you you should supply that demand — but maybe this company doesn't want to supply that demand yet, because it could change, or they just cannot handle the capacity of it.15:43

A trainee's paraphrase — "it's like a window where they ignore everything outside of it" — was confirmed on the call.

This is consistent with the vendor, which frames the same boundary from the supply side: "Within the order horizon, supply is planned in the form of planned orders. Within the master-planning horizon, supply is planned in the form of a supply plan" — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Planning Concepts — "Supply planned by order planning and master planning; item master plan" (p. 17).

3.4 Horizons are entered as days; the dates come from the scenario

cprpd1100m000's Horizons tab takes each horizon as a number of days and displays the resulting date beside it. Captured values for BAM-CHOP-PAIR: Order Horizon 10 days → 9/4/2026; Planning Horizon 365 days → 7/9/2027; Forecast Time Fence 0 days → 8/19/2026; Production Schedules 0 days → 8/20/2026; Operations Horizon 9999 days → 8/20/2032; Time Fence 0.00 days with Use Time Fence selected.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file

This matches the vendor exactly: "A horizon in Enterprise Planning is defined by a figure that indicates a number of workdays" — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Planning Concepts — "Horizons" (p. 18).

The dates the Item Order Plan displays are then shaped by the scenario's period settings, not by the horizon alone — these dates are pulling in the scenario time periods10:31. The scenario in use throughout is ACT, "Actual Plan (Day, Weekly basis)". Changing the period settings requires Initialize, Roll, and Update Scenario before the change takes effect, which the session demonstrated after LN prompted for it (13:08).

Warning

An eight-day order horizon resolved to a date two days further out than expected, and the discrepancy was not explained on the call. Counting from 2026-08-20, the trainer expected 8/27 or 8/28 and could not reconcile which — I don't know why it's having that two-day discrepancy12:11, and later why isn't it doing it right… I have to look at this later14:49. Workday counting, the scenario's period boundaries, and the calendar are all plausible causes and none was confirmed. Not documented for this release.

3.5 Running MRP, and why components need their own planning data

With the demand inside the horizon, MRP was run and produced a planned order to cover the shortfall. The point made next matters more: not only does it do that, it also includes the materials required from your BOM17:26 — but it only did so for the component that had planning data of its own. BAM-ROD had none; it was given plan data and planning was rerun, so now it's planning two items on that instead of one18:18.

The result reads as a chain across two sessions. On the parent's plan, the planned production order is supply. Open the component's own Item Order Plan and the same planned production order is demand, met by a planned purchase order. The plan production order that was supply for the main item is demand for the component19:06.

Planned Orders cprrp1100m000 is where results are actually reviewed, because normally you wouldn't run MRP for a single item, you would run it for all the plannable items22:55. The captured session shows the environment's existing planned orders across many items — order types Planned Purchase Order and Planned Production Order, with Order Quantity, Routing, Bill of Material and Required Date columns.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file

Two framings from this part are worth carrying into any future guide:

  • Planned orders are suggestions, not instructions. These are only suggestions… the actual planners know their data better than we do23:11. The worked example: a suggestion to buy 101 makes no sense where the supplier sells in batches of 100, so the planner buys 200. The vendor supports this as a configurable constraint rather than a judgement call — "You can specify that the ordered quantity must be a multiple of 100 units. If you have a requirement for 530 units, LN generates an order for 600 units", set in Items - Ordering tcibd2100m000 among others — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Planning Concepts — "Lot sizing" (p. 21).
  • A planned order becomes real by being transferred. From here you transfer, you actualize the order23:46. cprrp1100m000's own help confirms the mechanism and names the destination: a planned order carries a flag for whether it "is ready for transfer to the execution level", and on transfer the information "is passed on to the Job Shop Control module and the Purchase Control module." — Infor LN help for Planned Orders cprrp1100m000, Field Information

3.6 Order pegging

Pegging was introduced as the answer to multi-level complexity — like the one I'm on right now, they have like 20 levels. So after an MRP run it's difficult to track and manage that. But that's what pegging is for19:51.

The tree captured is Order Pegging cprrp0740m200, reached as Pegging - Upstream:

Sales Order 100000667-10 | NA1 BAM-CHOP-PAIR | Free | 5 of 5 ea | 08-27-2026
├─ Stock | ADNA01 | NA1 BAM-CHOP-PAIR | 1 of 1 ea
└─ Planned Production Order 000000061 | NA1 BAM-CHOP-PAIR | Planned | 4 of 4 ea | 08-27-2026
   └─ Planned Production Order 000000061-1 | NA1 BAM-ROD | Planned | 8 of 8 ea | 08-27-2026
      ├─ Stock | ADNA02 | NA1 BAM-ROD | 2 of 2 ea
      └─ Planned Purchase Order 000000346 | NA1 BAM-ROD | Planned | 6 of 6 ea | 08-27-2026

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file

The tree reconciles against the BOM recorded on 2026-08-18 — BAM-ROD at quantity 2 per pair — since four pairs require eight rods, of which two are in stock and six must be bought. `BAM-SLEEVE` does not appear, consistent with only one component having been given planning data during the session.

Upstream and downstream were both demonstrated and both are real commands: upstream is what will supply, downstream is… the demand that drove this order21:21. The vendor documents them on the same session: "To start the graphical browser from the Planned Orders (cprrp1100m000) session, on the appropriate menu, click Pegging - Upstream or Pegging - Downstream" — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Order Based Planning — "Browsing order pegging relationships" (p. 115).

The red-means-demand, green-means-supply convention stated on the call (20:32) is a reading of the tree's own icons. It is on-screen behaviour with no vendor statement found; treat it as observed, not defined.

Note

The narration did not mention a prerequisite the vendor treats as mandatory. Multi-level pegging is only browsable once pegging relations have been generated — either by selecting Update Pegging Relations in Generate Order Planning cprrp1210m000, or by running Generate Pegging Relations cprrp0240m000 — and LN only generates them out to the Pegging Horizon set in Planning Parameters cprpd0100m000. The tree resolved on the call without this being discussed, which suggests the environment already has it configured, but that was not shown. the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Order Based Planning — "Pegging for planned orders" (p. 113–114).

Shown 2026-08-25, and the answer is stronger than "already configured". The next session in the series captured cprrp1220m000's full option set, where Update Pegging Relations is selected _and_ read-only — the source on file. Its help states "You can modify this check box only if the `Update Pegging Relations during Order Planning` field in the Scenarios (cprpd4100m000) session is `User Specified`", so the disabled state means the ACT scenario is not set to User Specified and pegging relations are rebuilt on every run regardless of operator intent. Nobody on the 2026-08-20 call could have turned it off, which is why the tree resolved without the prerequisite being discussed. See planned-order-transfer-training_2026-08-25.md §3.5.

3.7 Master planning and the planning horizon

Master planning was covered as the other side of it that's not used as often23:54, with explicit advice to learn MRP first.

The mechanism as demonstrated:

  1. Master Plan is a check box on cprpd1100m000. Until it is selected, the Planning Horizon field is disabled — you got your order horizon and planning, but it's disabled. Why? Because master planning is not enabled24:17.
  2. With it selected, the planning horizon becomes separately maintainable — 365 days against the order horizon's 10 — "so now you can have a separate horizon maintained outside of MRP."
  3. The item master plan must then be initialized before it exists: every time you turn on the master plan, you got to initialize the master plan… so right now, see, it's just blank, doesn't exist26:07. This is the Initialize, Roll, and Update Scenario command, visible as a toolbar button on Item Master Plan cprmp2101m000.
  4. Generating then produces a production plan inside the item master plan, in the scenario's time buckets — this is why they call them buckets27:35.

The captured Item Master Plan shows the two horizons having genuinely diverged — Order Horizon `9/4/2026`, Planning Horizon `7/9/2027` — over a bucketed grid whose rows are Demand Forecast, Extra Demand, Special Demand, Nonconsumed Forecast, Independent Demand, Dependent Demand, Production Plan, Production Orders, Distribution Orders and Purchase Plan.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-20 — the source on file

The purpose given was capacity and long-lead-time planning: anticipating demand far enough out to know "if we're capable of fulfilling this demand further out" — materials lead times and storage space, factory capacity, and letting order-takers know if they can promise these delivery dates29:17.

3.8 Where narration and vendor sources diverge

Two claims need separating from the rest, because both concern the same pair of horizons and only one of them survives its source unchanged.

Confirmed. If master planning is not used, then the planning horizon will always equal the order horizon24:03. This is stated almost word for word by the vendor: "If the item does not have an item master plan, the planning horizon is equal to the order horizon." — Infor LN help for Item Order Plan cprrp0520m000, Field Information, Planning Horizon. It is also visible on screen twice over: with master planning off, both horizons read 8/20/2026 and later 8/28/2026 together; with it on, they diverge to 9/4/2026 and 7/9/2027.

Imprecise. There's two horizons. The order horizon is for MRP. The planning horizon is for master planning09:51. The vendor does not treat these as a pair of siblings. The planning horizon is the total, and it "consists of an order horizon and/or a master-planning horizon"; the horizon that is specifically for master planning is the master-planning horizon, a sub-horizon, not the planning horizon — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Planning Concepts — "Major horizons and time fences" (p. 19).

The practical consequence in cprpd1100m000 is the same, which is why the shorthand works on a training call: the planning-horizon field is the one you extend to plan further out under master planning. But the shorthand should not be written into a guide, because it makes the planning horizon sound like it excludes the order horizon when the vendor defines it as containing it.

Warning

The vendor's third horizon has no matching field on the screen as deployed. cpordplanug names an order horizon, a master-planning horizon, and a planning horizon that contains them; cprpd1100m000's Horizons tab exposes only Order Horizon and Planning Horizon. Whether the master-planning horizon is simply the span between the two, or a field held elsewhere, is not established by anything in the folder. Not documented for this release.

Decisions made

  1. MRP is the priority for the trainees; master planning is context. Stated twice and repeated in the closing instructions — MRP is the number one thing that you should know and learn, but master plan is good to know too24:51, and focus on MRP first27:05.
  2. The bamboo family stays the training vehicle for planning, continuing from the 2026-08-18 walkthrough rather than starting a fresh item set.
  3. Michelle's plant-3 item is to be cleaned of old demand before MRP is rerun against it — a separate workstream discussed in the standup, to be settled in a meeting half an hour later (01:03). Not part of this training.

Methodology and process notes

  • A controlled experiment beats an explanation, and it survives into the library as two frames. The order-horizon lesson was taught by moving one date and refreshing — demand present, demand gone, demand back. Because both states were captured on the same session with the horizon visible in the same block, the pair of screenshots proves the mechanism without needing the narration at all. Where a session demonstrates a cause, capture the before and the after; either one alone is just a screen.
  • A training session is the cheapest source of "what a session actually looks like" for sessions nobody has exported help for. Five of the seven frames filed here are the only record in the folder of their session's field layout. That does not make them a substitute for help text — a screenshot shows a value and never tells you what it means (§2.2) — but it does mean the export list can now be written from observed field names rather than guesses.
  • Verify the confident-sounding claim, not just the hedged one. The two horizon claims in section 3.8 were delivered with equal confidence in the same minute; one is near-verbatim vendor text and the other conflates two defined terms. Neither could be sorted by tone. The only thing that separated them was reading cpordplanug p. 19 and cprrp0520m000's Field Information side by side.
  • Record what the narration left out, not only what it got wrong. The pegging prerequisite in section 3.6 is not an error — nothing false was said — but a guide written from the transcript alone would produce an empty tree for anyone whose environment had not already generated pegging relations.
  • Recording: the source on file — the raw video stays local and never commits; the manifest carries its checksum and lists the transcript derivatives.
