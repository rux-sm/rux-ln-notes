Order to Cash Run-through — Review

Draft Review Updated 2026-09-08

Read the summary

Objective

An unscripted coaching session in which the trainee drove a complete order-to-cash run — sales order to demand, MRP to planned orders, transfer to a production order, material issue, receipt, inspection, shipment — with a second consultant guiding and a third participant attending his first meeting.

The declared subject is not where the evidence is. The first twenty minutes are a lot-sizing laboratory: Order Quantity Increment and Safety Stock were changed one at a time and the plan regenerated after each, producing a controlled before/after pair on a single variable. That is the most rigorous evidence in this library for how an order quantity is arrived at.

Key topics discussed

3.1 The order-interval finding, reached independently a second time

The trainee opened by recounting, unprompted, the resolution of the 20/20/10 planned-order split: "I finally figured out that it was all due to the, what's it called, items ordering... that's where you set the intervals. And at the time, the intervals were set to zero, so no orders were getting combined at all."

This library had already settled that on 2026-09-05 — the source on file §1.1. What this recording adds is not the mechanism but its independent rediscovery by a second person from the environment itself: the finding was not read back off this library, it was arrived at again.

Not documented for this release.

3.2 Safety stock is demand, stated plainly

Two statements, both worth keeping because no filed source says either:

"Safety stock is, you have to have inventory mandatory in your company. When you get below safety stock it generates a demand order automatically."
"There's no demand driving it — and safety stock is considered demand. Otherwise you need a production order asking for that part, or a sales order requesting inventory to ship."

The second is the load-bearing one. It names safety stock as one of exactly three demand sources the consultant recognises — safety stock, a production order's material requirement, and a sales order — and it predicts a result the group then produced deliberately: with safety stock zeroed and nothing outstanding, a planning run generates nothing at all.

Not documented for this release.

3.3 The increment laboratory: one variable, two frames, 45 becomes 50

The most rigorous evidence in this recording, and it is settled by frame rather than narration.

So the arithmetic is fully sourced. Safety stock 50 against on-hand 5 is a requirement of 45; at an increment of 1 the plan orders exactly that; at 25 it rounds up to the next whole increment above the requirement, giving 50. The consultant stated it in the same terms at 00:18:44: "it's going to take whatever your safety stock is, your increment is 25, so it wants you to buy 50 even though you have 5 in stock because the five doesn't divide into the 25 piece increment."

This is the first observed increment result this library has. the source on file §1 notes the help carries four worked lot-sizing examples; every prior capture of the field read the deployed 1.0000, which rounds nothing and therefore demonstrates nothing.

Note

_Planned Available_ reproduces exactly on both frames. On-hand 5 plus supply 45 gives 50; plus supply 50 gives 55. ATP moves with it here, unlike the 2026-08-04 frame in the source on file §4 where ATP stood still across five rows — consistent with there being one supply row and no competing demand, and it does not reopen the ATP anomaly recorded there.

3.4 The inheritance chain, and the field that would not change

Setting safety stock to zero on the item appeared to do nothing:

"Is it using global? Yes, it's using global. And see how the safety stock on that, on the site, is 50 — so it didn't zero it out."

The same frame that carries the increment carries this. The tcibd2600m000 capture's Sites subtab shows one row — site CSADNA01, PCG CSAD NA Site 1 Manufacturi Houston,TX, Order Method `Lot for Lot`, warehouse ADNA01 FINISHED GOODS, _Safety Stock_ `50.0000`, and _Use Global Item Ordering_ selected.

Two things follow. The inheritance chain this library documents for Order Interval governs _Safety Stock_ too; and Use Global Item Ordering is visible on the site row itself, so the frame shows both the flag and the value it defers to at once. The increment field was separately observed greyed out until the level was changed — "because use global" — which makes a greyed field the environment's own statement of which level owns the value.

The enterprise-planning homework's Sitting 3 teaches exactly this reading and can now cite a captured instance.

3.6 Completing an operation without issuing its material

A real error, hit live and diagnosed on camera. The trainee reported an operation complete, then found nothing had been issued:

"You basically completed an operation of building the item without the items, because you didn't release any warehousing."

The repair was to take the production order number into the warehousing order, issue the material, then advise and release it. The order of operations the group settled on is: release material to warehousing, issue it, then report the operation complete — and LN did not prevent the wrong order.

The first live instance in this library of a mandatory freeze being enforced. the source on file records that the Freeze Mandatory reading SG-ship-from-stock phase 5 rests on came from the shipment rather than from a line, and marks the session uncaptured. This corroborates the behaviour and still does not capture the line.

3.9 Scenarios as the outer horizon

Volunteered by the trainee and confirmed by the consultant:

Consistent with the source on file, and it adds a containment claim the concept does not make — that the scenario's horizon bounds the item horizons inside it. 3.14 The rest of the execution chain, narrated only

The run continued past the frames above and is recorded here without evidence, as leads:

  • Transfer is available from Item Order Plan's own line-level Actions, not only from Planned Orders — "drill into the line… and then under Actions, still have Transfer."
  • Material issue precedes reporting an operation. The repair sequence was: production order number ➔ warehouse orders ➔ advise ➔ release, after which "it went from ADNA02 to the work center."
  • Inspection then put-away. Drill into the receipt line ➔ References ➔ Warehouse Inspections ➔ approve or reject, which "changes the status to put away and then that would allow you to close out the production order."
  • A sales order does not close until it is processed. "The reason it's not popping up is because we haven't processed the sales order" — then Process Delivered Orders, then check the status at line level.
  • Financials is the step after, and this team does not do it. "The last part is financials… we don't do it at all, barely, that's what I've been told." Recorded as a scope boundary, not a gap.

3.11 Navigation was the trainee's only real difficulty

Asked directly by an attendee where he struggled, the trainee answered twice that it was not the process:

"I couldn't remember where to go. It's just finding the right sessions." … "Sometimes I forget their tab is in the line level."

Recorded because it names the same two failure modes twice — locating a session, and looking at header level for a line-level control (§3.8) — and because it is the clearest statement in any recording here of what this library is for. The advice given was to avoid keyboard shortcuts until the navigation is solid, against another colleague's advice to use them; the caption attributes it to the consultant while the sentence quotes the consultant in the third person, so the speaker is not certain.

Note

Presence proves a conjunction; absence proves nothing about it. Every undecidable row above is undecidable in the same direction — the field is not visible, and any single clause failing would hide it, as would a scrolled grid or the wrong tab. That asymmetry is why the sweep is cheap when it works and silent when it does not, and it is the reason to capture a form's full width rather than the part you came for.

Decisions made

  1. _Order Quantity Increment_ rounds the order up to the next whole increment above the requirement — a 45 requirement at an increment of 25 yields 50. Captured on both sides of the change.
  2. Safety stock counts as demand; with safety stock zeroed and nothing outstanding, a planning run correctly produces nothing.
  3. Ordering data inherits for _Safety Stock_ as well as _Order Interval_, and a greyed field identifies the owning level.
  4. Material must reach warehousing before an operation is reported complete; LN does not enforce the order.
  5. The shipment freeze is mandatory in this environment and is performed at line level.

Methodology and process notes

  • A controlled before/after on one variable is worth more than either frame alone. §3.3 is the strongest configuration evidence in this library because the two Item Order Plan frames are identical in every column but one, and the frame that changed the input is captured too. Neither frame on its own would establish the rounding rule; the pair does, and it took no extra capture effort — only capturing before as well as after.
  • A confident spoken number was wrong against the screen it described. §3.12 is the strongest case in this library for capturing rather than transcribing: the guide states a BOM ratio aloud, specifically and without hedging, and the frame in front of him contradicts it. A transcript records what someone believed, not what the system holds — and a review written from one would have manufactured a contradiction against this library's own BOM.
  • The recording was very nearly written up from narration alone. The first pass of this review recorded every §3 finding as an unverified claim and listed five captures as action items, because no frames had been pulled. Five frames converted four of those findings to fact and removed three issues that were about to be opened. The lesson is the cost asymmetry: pulling a frame is minutes, and re-running an experiment to capture what you already watched is a session.
  • A field name spoken on a call is not a field name — until the client shows it is. Exception Handling returns zero matches across every exported help topic and was written up as speech; the expanded menu then showed it is a real submenu. Principle 3 in one step: the vendor documentation is silent and only the environment can answer.
  • The most valuable segment preceded the declared subject. The session was billed as an order-to-cash run; the evidence is in the twenty minutes of parameter changes before it. A scripted walkthrough does not produce that.
  • Two transcriptions of one recording are not redundant when one carries speakers. The platform's own caption export attributes all 855 filed cues and settled a question faster-whisper could not: who was teaching. The first draft of §2 had the guiding role assigned from narration and would have been wrong about it — the session was set up for one person to guide and a different person did. Where a claim's weight depends on who made it, an unattributed transcript is not enough, and principle 4 rests exactly there.
  • Recording: the source on file. The filed recording is trimmed — it begins at 00:02:24 of the source, removing pre-meeting small talk that contains no LN content. The manifest records the trim; the reason is held outside this repository. The raw video stays local and never commits.
