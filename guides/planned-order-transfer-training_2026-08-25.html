Objective

Carry order planning forward from the 2026-08-20 session to the step that was named there but never walked: turning a planned order into a real one. The 2026-08-20 review recorded the mechanism as narration — "from here you transfer, you actualize the order" — and cited cprrp1100m000's help for the destination. This session performs it, on the same bamboo family, and LN prints the result twice.

Along the way the session repairs an item that planning had been silently ignoring, and hits a transfer failure whose error message turns out to document the rule that caused it.

Key topics discussed

3.1 A planned order is not an order, and that is the point

The framing given for the whole session was that planned orders are deliberately not real, and that this is a feature rather than a stage to hurry through:

A plan order isn't an actual order yet. It's not an open order. It's just a planned order — which allows you to rerun MRP over and over and over again, you know, without worrying about anything. Because the actualization of the plan orders, that's what happens when you transfer.08:26

Restated a moment later as a working method — if anything changes, you know, any materials change, you can just re-plan, re-plan, re-plan — until you have the orders that you need, well then that's when you can transfer them08:54.

The vendor states the same boundary as the session's purpose: Transfer Order Planning cppat1210m000 is used "to transfer planned orders from Enterprise Planning to the execution level", and the destinations are exact — "Planned production orders and production plans are translated to production order in the Job Shop Control module in Manufacturing. Planned purchase orders and purchase plans are translated to purchase orders in Procurement." — Infor LN help for Transfer Order Planning cppat1210m000, session description.

This corroborates and sharpens the 2026-08-20 review's §3.5, which recorded the same idea from narration alone.

3.2 What an order type is decided by

Two planned purchase orders and two planned production orders sat side by side in the plan, and the trainee asked why. The answer given was the item's own setting: the reason that these are planned purchase and not planned production is what you set in the item master — default supply source10:59, repeated at the transfer step as it's not going to create sales orders, it's going to create purchase or production depending on the supply source of that item26:31.

The environment shows the field where the narration says it is. On Item tcibd0501m100, the Sites subtab carries a Default Supply Source column, reading Purchase for BAM-SLEEVE at site CSADNA01. The parent BAM-CHOP-PAIR reads Job Shop in the Actual Supply Source field of its own Item Order Plan, against Purchase for BAM-ROD.

3.3 An item with no planning cluster is skipped in silence

This was the most useful failure in the session, because nothing announced it.

BAM-SLEEVE produced no planned orders while its siblings did. The cause was found by inspection rather than by any message: you don't have a planning cluster for BAM-SLEEVE13:34. The link was then explained from the screen the trainee already had open — look on the top, in your header level at your Item Order Plan, see how it says Plan Item `NA1`? That's tied to your planning cluster17:31.

The repair, described as it was made: all I did was add a line which was `NA1`, and I just had to input the site data which was `CSADNA01`, and it has a warehouse tied to that which is `ADNA02`18:58. On tcibd0501m100 the destination is the Clusters subtab, which sits alongside Sites, Enterprise Units, Purchase Offices, Sales Offices and Service Offices.

LN training environment, verified 2026-08-25 — the source on file

The consequence was stated as a prediction and is worth keeping as procedure: we're probably gonna have to run MRP again, because when it ran the first time it probably didn't pick up the demand for this item since it was missing the planning cluster19:15.

This is the second recording to reach the same conclusion by a different route. On 2026-08-20 an item could not be planned until planning data existed at all; here an item that had planning data was still skipped because the cluster line was absent. Both point at the vendor's rule that a planning cluster "represents one or more business locations for which planning processes are performed" and that "if the multisite concepts are activated, planning clusters and sites are mandatory" — the Infor LN Order Planning user guide, Planning Clusters (p. 39, and p. 22 for the segment).

The Items - Planning cprpd1100m000 Horizons tab was captured again, this time for BAM-ROD with Master Plan cleared — the complement of the 2026-08-20 capture, which had it selected. That answers a question the 2026-08-20 review left open. Its §3.6 recorded that multi-level pegging requires pegging relations to have been generated, that this was never discussed on that call, and that the tree resolving anyway "suggests the environment already has it configured, but that was not shown". 3.7 Why rerunning planning is safe

The trainee asked whether rerunning would disturb existing orders. The answer was that regeneration replaces what it previously suggested and leaves real orders alone: it's gonna rerun planning, it's gonna regenerate orders, and any old orders, if they're obsolete, if they're not needed anymore, this should erase those… and the reason they're staying the same is because we didn't transfer any of them to actual order19:41.

The vendor states the rule precisely, and it is narrower than the narration: "LN always removes all previously generated planned orders with `Planned` order status before it starts a new simulation. Previously generated planned orders with `Confirmed` order status are retained." — Infor LN help for Generate Order Planning Item cprrp1220m000, Field Information, Remove Firm Planned Orders. Three statuses are in play, not two: Planned is swept every run, Confirmed survives, and Firm Planned is swept only when that check box is selected — it was cleared here.

The narration's "if they're obsolete, if they're not needed anymore" is therefore the right intuition with the wrong mechanism. LN does not assess obsolescence; it deletes every Planned order and rebuilds. The visible result is identical whenever the rebuild reproduces the same suggestions, which is why the shorthand survives a training call — but a guide should not repeat it, because it implies orders can persist across a run on merit.

Methodology and process notes

  • An error message can be a better source than a screenshot of the field it points at. The buy-from failure named its own two-place lookup rule in one sentence — "neither valid Item - BP data is present, nor a BP is linked to Item Purchase Data" — which is more than either screen shows on its own, and more than any filed help says about this dependency. When a session fails during a walk, capture the report before dismissing it; it is the one moment LN explains itself unprompted. This is the same lesson learned by not capturing a dialog in 2026-08-18.
  • A greyed-out control is evidence about configuration, not just about the screen. Update Pegging Relations being selected and disabled resolves a question the previous review had to leave open, because the help explains exactly which scenario setting disables it. A tick alone would have proved less. Capture the enabled/disabled state, not only the value.
  • Capture the same tab twice in opposite states and the pair proves a rule. The Horizons tab with Master Plan cleared is only interesting beside the 2026-08-20 capture with it selected; together they demonstrate the planning-horizon rule without needing either narration. This is the paired-frame method §3.3 of the 2026-08-20 review already named, applied across two recordings five days apart rather than within one.
  • The transcript needs a correction key before anyone reads it unaccompanied. Five mis-hearings recur and two of them look like real terms: "automotive plant" is Item Order Plan throughout (10:14, 10:21, 26:11), "multi-level bombs" is multi-level BOMs (24:51), "MRT" is MRP (01:28), "Ellen's" is LN is (30:23), and "LA" is LN (32:13). A reader who does not know the product would take "automotive plant" for a site name.
  • Say which half of a step was walked. This session performs the transfer for planned purchase orders only. Recording it as "the transfer was walked" would have made the production half look closer to done than it is.
